How to read this chart
  • The colored line is Bitcoin's weekly price. Its color is the Cycle Heat Score at that time: blue = cool, gray = neutral, orange = warm, red = hot. Hover or tap the chart for the price and score of any week.
  • The gray corridor runs from the orange floor line up to the dotted ceiling line, around the dashed trend line. All three come from one long-term curve fitted to Bitcoin's whole history. Price has rarely left this corridor, but nothing guarantees it stays inside.
  • The light blue band shows where price was in the previous two halving cycles at the same point in the cycle. After "now" it continues, scaled down because each cycle has peaked less far above trend. It is a shape guide only, not a forecast: last cycle it pointed far above the actual 2025 top.
  • The faint purple fan is the model's range of outcomes to the end of 2028. If the model is right, price lands in the darker middle about half the time (25-75%) and inside the whole fan about 90% of the time (5-95%). It widens further out because the future is less certain. Its shading stops at the floor line for readability.
  • Heat zones (cool, neutral, warm, hot) say how stretched price and on-chain data look compared with all of history. Hot readings have clustered near past cycle tops and cool ones near past bottoms.
  • Track record: in the backtest the score peaked around 93 at the 2013 and 2017 tops but only about 65 to 68 at the 2021 and 2025 tops, just above the hot line, which was itself set from those tops. Peaks have come down, so a top can come without the score going "hot".
  • What this is not: a trade timing signal or buy/sell advice. It is a slow gauge of where we are in the cycle that changes over weeks and months. For entries and exits, pair it with your own short-term tools (moving averages, RSI and so on).

Not financial advice. Model output from public data, not a prediction or a recommendation to buy or sell. Past cycles may not repeat.

Method technical details

  1. Own index price: median of the latest trade/ticker prices from Coinbase, Kraken and Bitstamp WebSockets. An exchange counts only if its last tick is under 15 s old, and one that is more than 0.5% away from the others is left out. A socket that goes silent for 10 s is reconnected, and every socket is checked again when the page becomes visible, regains focus or comes back online (phones freeze background tabs); the exchange REST endpoints are polled meanwhile. Fallback: if fewer than 2 sockets deliver a price within ~5 s (e.g. a network that blocks WebSockets), the page switches to HTTPS polling every 10 s of the three exchanges plus Gemini, Binance.US, Crypto.com, Blockchain.com and CoinGecko (all public, CORS-enabled, different domains), with the same median and 0.5% outlier rule; the header then says live (polling every 10s) instead of live (streaming). Blocks fall back from mempool.space to blockstream.info to blockchain.info. If nothing has priced for 15 s the price is marked STALE; if nothing was reachable at all, the header shows the last daily close in gray and the feed-check line says which connections failed.
  2. Signals are recomputed every second with the live price as today's daily close: Mayer Multiple (price / 200-day MA), price / 200-week (1400-day) MA, price / my power-law trend fit (refit including today), Pi Cycle ratio (111DMA / 2×350DMA), MVRV Z (live market cap = price × supply; realized cap from the latest daily print), plus halving-cycle timing. Address activity (daily) and Google search interest (monthly) only change when their sources publish.
  3. Wikipedia attention (display only): daily views of the English Wikipedia "Bitcoin" article (Wikimedia pageviews API, users only, since July 2015). Heat = percentile of the 30-day average against the prior 8 years. It is shown next to the signals but is not part of either score; the pre-registered scores keep Google Trends.
  4. Heat = percentile of today's value against every prior daily value since 2010. Timing heat = 100 at the average halving-to-peak day of past tops, bell-curve fall-off.
  5. Cycle Heat Score = plain average of the 8 heats. Zones: hot = lowest score seen around any past top; warm = hot − 15; neutral 40+; cool below 40.
  6. Projection fan: each day the power-law trend is refit on all data; the bands come from the model's own past forecast errors at each horizon (weighted toward recent cycles, half-life 4 years), so they widen the further out you look. No single price target. On the chart it is drawn faintly, behind the forward cycle path. Fan shading is cut off at the floor line for readability; the model's 5% quantile can sit below the floor, and the floor is not guaranteed.
  7. Cycle band / forward cycle path (display only): before now, the range of the two prior halving cycles' deviations from the trend at the same day since the halving (±10 days, 15-day smoothing). After now, the same construction with each prior cycle's deviation scaled down by ρcycles back, where ρ (≈0.78) is the average shrink of successive cycle peaks above trend over the completed cycles (the v3-candidate "dampened" rule). The first 8 weeks blend from the past band so there is no step at now. It is a shape guide from past cycles, not a forecast, and it is not part of either score. In the 2024 cycle even the dampened path ran well above the actual 2025 top.
  8. Score v2 (pre-registered 2026-10-25): heat = percentile vs the prior 8 years only; signals grouped into 5 independent families (price structure, on-chain valuation, network usage, attention, timing), score = mean of families; zones fixed at 80/65/40. Its code is frozen by sha256 and logged daily next to v1. See ROBUSTNESS.md.
  9. Backtest (walk-forward): the score reached 92-93 at the 2013 and 2017 tops but only ~65-68 at the 2021 and 2025 tops; it fell to 12-25 at every cycle low. It is a description of how stretched the market is versus history, not a sell timer.
