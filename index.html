How to read this chart
  • The colored line is Bitcoin's weekly price. Its color is the Cycle Heat Score at that time: blue = cool, gray = neutral, orange = warm, red = hot. The blue tag on the right axis and the dot at the end of the line are the live price; the small status next to "Reset view" says whether it is live, polling, STALE or the last daily close. Hover or tap the chart for the price and score of any week.
  • The gray corridor runs from the orange floor line up to the dotted ceiling line, around the dashed trend line. All three come from one long-term curve fitted to Bitcoin's whole history. Price has rarely left this corridor, but nothing guarantees it stays inside.
  • The light blue band shows where price was in the previous two halving cycles at the same point in the cycle. After "now" it continues in the same style, built from the same past cycles but scaled down because each cycle has peaked less far above trend. It shows the next possible top (around 2029, marked with a small dot) and the fall after it (around 2030, second dot); hover or tap for the month and the band's middle and range. It runs only as far as the previous cycles' data reaches (late 2030 for now, one more day each day). The band on the chart is recomputed daily; the copy frozen on 2026-10-25 is the pre-registered claim graded in the scorecard. Last cycle it pointed far above the actual 2025 top, so the test may well fail.
  • The graded forecast range is not on this chart. The model's locked range of outcomes (the "fan", frozen on 2026-10-25, to the end of 2028) is the part that gets graded. It is shown as a small table in the scorecard section, next to its public grades, so it is not confused with the blue shape guide here.
  • Heat zones (cool, neutral, warm, hot) say how stretched price and on-chain data look compared with all of history. Hot readings have clustered near past cycle tops and cool ones near past bottoms.
  • Track record: in the backtest the score peaked around 93 at the 2013 and 2017 tops but only about 65 to 68 at the 2021 and 2025 tops, just above the hot line, which was itself set from those tops. Peaks have come down, so a top can come without the score going "hot".
  • What this is not: a trade timing signal or buy/sell advice. It is a slow gauge of where we are in the cycle that changes over weeks and months. For entries and exits, pair it with your own short-term tools (moving averages, RSI and so on).

Method technical details: price index, signals, score, forecast range, cycle band
  1. Own index price: median of the latest trade/ticker prices from Coinbase, Kraken and Bitstamp WebSockets. An exchange counts only if its last tick is under 15 s old, and one that is more than 0.5% away from the others is left out. A socket that goes silent for 10 s is reconnected, and every socket is checked again when the page becomes visible, regains focus or comes back online (phones freeze background tabs); the exchange REST endpoints are polled meanwhile. Fallback: if fewer than 2 sockets deliver a price within ~5 s (e.g. a network that blocks WebSockets), the page switches to HTTPS polling every 10 s of the three exchanges plus Gemini, Binance.US, Crypto.com, Blockchain.com and CoinGecko (all public, CORS-enabled, different domains), with the same median and 0.5% outlier rule; the status next to the chart then says live (polling every 10s) instead of live. Blocks fall back from mempool.space to blockstream.info to blockchain.info. If nothing has priced for 15 s the price is marked STALE; if nothing was reachable at all, the status shows last close in gray and the chart and scores use the last daily close. Its (i) note lists each source's price and connection state and which connections failed.
  2. Signals are recomputed every second with the live price as today's daily close: Mayer Multiple (price / 200-day MA), price / 200-week (1400-day) MA, price / my power-law trend fit (refit including today), Pi Cycle ratio (111DMA / 2×350DMA), MVRV Z (live market cap = price × supply; realized cap from the latest daily print), plus halving-cycle timing. Address activity (daily) and Google search interest (monthly) only change when their sources publish.
  3. Crowd euphoria (display only): a contrarian gauge of trader excitement, 0-100. It is the average of the 7-day average Crypto Fear & Greed Index (alternative.me, daily since Feb 2018) and a 0-100 score of the BTC perpetual-futures funding rate (what leveraged longs pay shorts; 7-day average, annualized; Deribit, with OKX as a backup). The funding score runs through 0 at -10% a year, 20 at 0%, 45 at 10% (about the normal level), 65 at 20%, 85 at 40% and 100 at 80%. Zones: calm under 25, normal 25-55, excited 55-75, euphoric 75 and up, chosen as display conventions, not fitted. It is not part of the Cycle Heat Score or any graded version; using it in a score would need a new locked version, reviewed no earlier than 2027-10-25.
  4. Heat = percentile of today's value against every prior daily value since 2010. Timing heat = 100 at the average halving-to-peak day of past tops, bell-curve fall-off.
  5. Cycle Heat Score = plain average of the 8 heats. Zones: hot = lowest score seen around any past top; warm = hot − 15; neutral 40+; cool below 40.
  6. Projection fan (the graded forecast range): each day the power-law trend is refit on all data; the bands come from the model's own past forecast errors at each horizon (weighted toward recent cycles, half-life 4 years), so they widen the further out you look. No single price target. It is not drawn on the main chart; the locked copy (frozen 2026-10-25, horizon Dec 31 2028) and its v3 version are shown as a table in the scorecard section, and the scorecard grades the daily 30/90/365-day forecasts. The model's 5% level can sit below the floor line, and the floor is not guaranteed.
  7. Cycle band / forward cycle path (pre-registered 2026-10-25): before now, the range of the two prior halving cycles' deviations from the trend at the same day since the halving (±10 days, 15-day smoothing). After now, the same construction with each prior cycle's deviation scaled down by ρcycles back, where ρ (≈0.78) is the average shrink of successive cycle peaks above trend over the completed cycles (the "dampened" rule). The first 8 weeks blend from the past band so there is no step at now. It is drawn to the end of 2030, together with the power-law corridor. The band on the chart is recomputed every day. The graded claim is the copy frozen on 2026-10-25: next top about 2029-09-25 (±91 days) at $355K-$653K, then a low about 2030-06-04 at $227K-$337K. Its code, its daily values and the grading rules are fixed by sha256 and timestamped (PREREG_BAND.md), and it is scored in the scorecard section against simple benchmarks and three competitors. It is not part of either Cycle Heat Score. In the 2024 cycle even the dampened path ran well above the actual 2025 top, and the test may well fail. Historical pattern test, not a price target.
  8. Score v2 (pre-registered 2026-10-25): heat = percentile vs the prior 8 years only; signals grouped into 5 independent families (price structure, on-chain valuation, network usage, attention, timing), score = mean of families; zones fixed at 80/65/40. Its code is frozen by sha256 and logged daily next to v1. See ROBUSTNESS.md.
  9. Backtest (walk-forward): the score reached 92-93 at the 2013 and 2017 tops but only ~65-68 at the 2021 and 2025 tops; it fell to 12-25 at every cycle low. It is a description of how stretched the market is versus history, not a sell timer.
Verify, cite and data sources frozen files, hashes, Bitcoin timestamps, DOI, raw data

Every graded rule and prediction is frozen in files whose SHA-256 hashes are published and timestamped on Bitcoin (OpenTimestamps), and a daily hash chain records the logs, so nothing can be quietly edited. Anyone can re-check it with free tools.
